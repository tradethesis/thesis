# Six futures for Thesis

Research and product proposal · 24 September 2026

These are editorial investment hypotheses, not verified bargains or recommendations to purchase today. Evidence that a change is happening does not establish that a particular company captures the economics, or that its current stock price leaves attractive upside. Each thesis needs those three questions kept separate.

## Recommendation

Maintain six durable research subjects, but feature only allocations that can actually be expressed and executed. Two subjects have relevant constituents already enabled in the local allowlist. Three need asset discovery and verification. Robotics should remain a research subject until suitable exposure is found. Do not pad a basket with unrelated stocks to make six purchase buttons or three holdings per card.

The headlines below are original editorial framing. The publishers linked below did not author or endorse these portfolios. All external sources were accessed on 24 September 2026; publication dates and observation periods must stay attached to evidence. An old source does not become a new development because we fetched it today.

## 1. Intelligence becomes a utility

- **ID:** `intelligence-as-a-utility`
- **Card copy:** Every business can rent intelligence. Who gets paid when it does?
- **Belief:** Useful AI becomes a recurring operating expense across businesses. The providers of compute and cloud distribution could collect continuing revenue as applications multiply.
- **Why someone backs it:** They expect AI to become everyday infrastructure and want exposure beyond choosing one app or model.
- **Possible underappreciated angle, not established mispricing:** Competition between applications could expand infrastructure consumption even when individual apps lose their edge. The open question is whether efficiency and price declines absorb those gains.
- **Economic exposure:** NVIDIA supplies compute; Microsoft and Amazon distribute cloud capacity and AI services. They have different roles and costs, not three identical claims on the same revenue. These are broad companies; none is a pure claim on AI adoption.
- **Local fit:** NVDAx, MSFTx and AMZNx are enabled in `EQUITY_ASSETS`. The existing `contracted-capex-pays-the-supplier-first` allocation is a candidate to attach, subject to editorial review. Preserve its actual version and weights; do not reuse its performance as a newly authored call. Other AI arguments can live alongside it without becoming more front-page products.
- **Evidence:** NVIDIA's [Q2 FY2027 release](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-second-quarter-fiscal-2027) reports substantial data-center demand. Microsoft's [FY2026 Q4 release](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast) reports Azure growth. Amazon's [Q2 2026 release](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-earnings-q2-2026-report) shows AWS growth alongside substantial AI-related capital expenditure and pressure on free cash flow. They support demand and the cost objection, not a conclusion about valuation.
- **Watch:** AI/cloud revenue where separately disclosed; cash generation after capital expenditure; utilization and customer concentration when reported. Do not label all Azure/AWS revenue as AI revenue.
- **Strong objection:** Adoption grows while competition, depreciation, custom chips or a high starting valuation prevent shareholder gains.
- **Review trigger:** Review the next two reported quarters for evidence that infrastructure spending is converting into durable cash generation. If companies do not disclose enough to isolate AI economics, label that question unresolved.
- **Boundary:** This concerns compute and cloud economics. Power equipment belongs to thesis 3; physical deployment belongs to thesis 4.

## 2. Wall Street moves onto internet rails

- **ID:** `finance-on-internet-rails`
- **Card copy:** Dollars and investments become assets that move at internet speed.
- **Belief:** More settlement, distribution and financial activity use onchain infrastructure. Selected public intermediaries could capture revenue from that transition.
- **Why someone backs it:** They already use stablecoins and believe the experience will spread beyond crypto-native users.
- **Possible underappreciated angle:** Adoption may arrive through familiar financial apps rather than everyone becoming a DeFi user. Whether listed intermediaries retain pricing power remains uncertain.
- **Economic exposure:** Circle supplies stablecoin infrastructure and earns reserve-related income; Coinbase provides access, trading and custody; Robinhood adds retail financial distribution. The latter two include substantial businesses unrelated to onchain settlement. This is a broad financial-distribution thesis, not a pure stablecoin-payment basket.
- **Local fit:** COINx, CRCLx and HOODx are enabled. Reuse the versioned basket behind `financial-activity-moves-onchain` only after confirming its current execution state. Its gift design is currently intentionally unfeatured: inspect why before changing visibility; do not resurrect it merely to fill a shelf.
- **Evidence:** Circle's [21 April 2025 Payments Network announcement](https://www.circle.com/pressroom/circle-announces-payments-network-to-transform-global-money-movement) describes a stablecoin settlement network for financial institutions. It establishes a product initiative, not current payment volume or profitable adoption across this basket. Check current filings for the revenue exposure of each proposed holding before publishing new claims.
- **Watch:** Stablecoin supply and payment usage with a stated methodology; reserve income and distribution costs; attributable custody/transaction revenue; retail adoption of onchain products. Raw blockchain volume includes activities that are not payments.
- **Strong objection:** The system moves onchain but banks, competing issuers or protocols capture the value. Lower rates or lower fees can reduce earnings despite higher activity.
- **Review trigger:** Two earnings reviews comparing adoption with attributable economics. Revenue definitions differ by company: do not invent a single comparable “onchain revenue” metric.
- **Boundary:** Stablecoins, tokenized settlement and retail access are subtopics here. Generic “crypto goes mainstream” is an alias or an argument, not a seventh product.

## 3. The AI race runs on electricity

- **ID:** `power-for-ai`
- **Card copy:** More intelligence needs more power, cooling, and equipment to connect it.
- **Belief:** Physical infrastructure constrains data-center expansion. Suppliers that turn planned compute into operating capacity could capture durable spending.
- **Why someone backs it:** It is a tangible way to express the AI buildout without selecting a model winner.
- **Possible underappreciated angle:** A chip order is not an operating data center. Electrical equipment, cooling and grid access can determine when capacity earns revenue.
- **Economic exposure candidates:** Eaton (ETN): electrical equipment; Vertiv (VRT): data-center power and thermal management. A generation leg such as Constellation (CEG) requires a separate current contract/economics review. This is a research list of underlying businesses, not a verified token list or allocation.
- **Local fit:** These are not enabled in the local allowlist. Verify issuer identity, exact Solana mint, rights, transfer restrictions and two-way routes before considering a buyable allocation. Never confuse an underlying ticker, a leveraged ETF with a similar ticker, and an xStock. No unverified mint should appear in executable seed data.
- **Evidence:** The IEA's [2025 Energy and AI executive summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary) projects data-center electricity consumption of around 945 TWh in 2030 in its base case; this is a scenario, not realized demand. Eaton's [Q2 2026 results](https://www.eaton.com/us/en-us/company/news-insights/news-releases/2026/eaton-reports-record-second-quarter-2026-results.html) identify data centers as a growth driver and report stronger electrical orders. Eaton also serves other end markets.
- **Watch:** Orders turning into revenue/cash; margins; cancellations; delivery constraints; announced versus energized capacity. Distinguish generation, distribution and cooling economics.
- **Strong objection:** Project delays, efficiency improvements, customers overbuilding, or a high valuation can overwhelm the spending story. A grid bottleneck can delay suppliers' revenue rather than raise it.
- **Review trigger:** Reassess if two reported quarters show order deterioration or weaker cash conversion while the headline AI spending narrative remains strong.
- **Boundary:** “AI needs power,” “the grid is the bottleneck,” and “cooling the AI boom” are perspectives here. A pure nuclear-energy claim is not automatically identical and needs its own causal review.

## 4. Robots clock in

- **ID:** `robots-clock-in`
- **Card copy:** Physical work starts becoming programmable.
- **Belief:** Automation expands through factories and warehouses where measurable productivity supports commercial deployment.
- **Why someone backs it:** They believe AI will affect the physical world and want a position in deployment, not just impressive demonstrations.
- **Possible underappreciated angle:** Repeat purchases, uptime and integration economics may matter more than humanoid appearance.
- **Economic exposure to research:** Commercial robot suppliers, industrial controls and integration providers with identifiable robotics economics. [Rockwell's 2025 annual report](https://www.rockwellautomation.com/content/dam/rockwell-automation/documents/pdf/company/about-us/ir/2025/ROK-AR-2025-Final.pdf) is a diligence starting point for industrial automation exposure, not proof that a specific stock is the right robotics investment. Recheck current corporate ownership before naming robotics divisions as public-stock exposure.
- **Local fit:** Research-only. No suitable direct robotics basket has been verified in this review. Do not manufacture one from NVDAx, AMZNx and TSLAx just because their products touch robotics; TSLAx is also disabled locally. Do not imply ownership of private robotics companies.
- **Evidence:** The IFR's [World Robotics 2025 release](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years), published 25 September 2025, reports 542,000 industrial installations in 2024. Industrial adoption does not prove commercial humanoid deployment.
- **Watch:** Paid deployments, repeat customers, disclosed utilization and service costs, operating margins, and backlog conversion. A video or pilot announcement is not commercial revenue.
- **Strong objection:** Integration costs and slow replacement cycles limit rollout. Productivity gains may accrue to customers rather than robot manufacturers.
- **Review trigger:** Review paid deployment and repeat-order evidence every quarter. If only demonstrations and pilots are available, leave commercial scale unproven.
- **Boundary:** Factory robots, warehouse automation and mobile robots are subtopics. Robotaxis and consumer humanoids are not interchangeable exposure.

## 5. Medicine rewrites appetite

- **ID:** `metabolic-medicine`
- **Card copy:** Metabolic treatment could become a much larger part of everyday healthcare.
- **Belief:** Effective metabolic medicines reach more eligible patients as access, delivery and manufacturing improve, creating a larger treatment market.
- **Why someone backs it:** A tangible change in healthcare with a demand driver separate from cloud spending.
- **Possible underappreciated angle:** Access, adherence and affordable delivery may matter as much as another efficacy headline. A broader market may come with lower net prices.
- **Economic exposure candidates:** Eli Lilly (LLY) and Novo Nordisk (NVO), subject to product-mix, patent, pricing and competition analysis. Two relevant holdings are preferable to an unrelated third healthcare stock.
- **Local fit:** Kraken's [xStocks catalogue](https://www.kraken.com/pro/xstocks) lists LLYx and NVOx. Neither is in the local allowlist. An external listing is not verified local tradeability; mint and route validation remains necessary.
- **Evidence:** The FDA's [8 March 2024 Wegovy announcement](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-treatment-reduce-risk-serious-heart-problems-specifically-adults-obesity-or) extends a specific indication to reducing cardiovascular risk in a defined patient group. Lilly's [pipeline, as of 5 August 2026](https://www.lilly.com/science/research-development/pipeline), provides current development context. Approval, trial evidence and commercial economics are different facts; do not turn them into a universal treatment claim.
- **Watch:** Access and reimbursement, net pricing, prescription persistence where reported, supply capacity, trial outcomes and competitive launches.
- **Strong objection:** Price compression, treatment discontinuation, safety issues, competition or valuation can outweigh patient growth.
- **Review trigger:** Reassess after material trial/regulatory events and each earnings cycle; weakening net prices and persistence despite rising prescriptions challenges the commercial case.
- **Boundary:** Do not rename this “longevity” or imply these companies solve aging. Do not market a pack as a comment on the recipient's body or health.

## 6. The internet needs a stronger immune system

- **ID:** `security-for-an-automated-world`
- **Card copy:** More software acting for us creates more identities and systems to protect.
- **Belief:** Expanding cloud use and automation increase security demands. Effective vendors could earn recurring spending for protecting endpoints, identities and workloads.
- **Why someone backs it:** They expect a more automated internet, but want exposure to the cost of protecting it rather than another compute basket.
- **Possible underappreciated angle:** Authorization and machine identities may become as important as protecting human users. This remains a hypothesis about demand and vendor capture.
- **Economic exposure candidates:** CrowdStrike (CRWD) for endpoint/cloud protection; Palo Alto Networks (PANW) and Fortinet (FTNT) as businesses for further product-mix research. Do not assume all sell equivalent identity protection or all have supported Solana tokens.
- **Local fit:** Kraken's [catalogue](https://www.kraken.com/pro/xstocks) lists CRWDx. It is not enabled locally; this review does not establish token availability for the other candidates. Research-only until a defensible, verified allocation exists. Never substitute Microsoft just to finish a three-stock pack without showing its indirect exposure.
- **Evidence:** CrowdStrike's [24 February 2026 threat report announcement](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/press-releases/2026-crowdstrike-global-threat-report/) reports increased AI-enabled adversary activity in its observations. This is a vendor's telemetry and interpretation, not a complete census of attacks or proof of future security budgets.
- **Watch:** Retention, new contract value where defined, margins, customer consolidation, and material reliability incidents. Attack counts are not vendor earnings.
- **Strong objection:** Buyers consolidate tools, incumbents bundle security, or vendors undermine trust through their own failures. Threat growth need not produce higher margins.
- **Review trigger:** Two earnings reviews showing worse retention/pricing or weaker cash generation despite demand claims should challenge the investment case.
- **Boundary:** Cyber defense is distinct from AI copyright indemnity, media fact-checking and content authenticity. A post about deepfakes cannot automatically support endpoint-security revenue.

## How to keep this catalogue honest

1. **Do not force six live investments.** Separate research availability from executable asset availability. Two eligible allocations plus four clearly marked research subjects are better than six fictional baskets. If the product already has useful other packs, preserve them outside this featured collection.
2. **Overlap is allowed and explained.** Four of these ideas relate to automation, so they are not six independent macro bets. They have different revenue mechanisms. Shared-demand risk exists even where ticker overlap is zero.
3. **Merge wording, not every related idea.** Equivalent claims become aliases; distinct reasoning becomes an argument; contradictory interpretations can coexist. Same holdings do not prove two arguments are identical.
4. **Keep three verdicts separate:** change in the world, company capture, valuation. Mark valuation unassessed unless an actual dated valuation comparison was performed. Never print “early,” “undervalued” or a success probability from semantic relevance.
5. **Content can change; positions cannot silently change.** Evidence and editorial interpretation evolve. A user's funded gift, order or holding remains pinned to its reviewed allocation. New evidence never causes an automatic rebalance.
6. **Start editorially.** Seed verified sources and review updates manually. An LLM can suggest which thesis a post relates to and whether it supports/challenges it; it cannot supply facts, endorse a holding, choose live weights or publish itself.
